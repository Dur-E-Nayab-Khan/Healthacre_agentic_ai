Type 2 Diabetes Management Guideline (Sample Reference Document)
==================================================================

1. Diagnosis
Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed via fasting plasma glucose >= 126 mg/dL,
HbA1c >= 6.5%, or a 2-hour plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL during an oral
glucose tolerance test, confirmed on repeat testing unless the patient
has unambiguous hyperglycemia symptoms.

2. First-line pharmacologic therapy
Metformin remains first-line therapy for most newly diagnosed patients
unless contraindicated (e.g. eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m^2, acute illness with
risk of lactic acidosis). Typical starting dose is 500mg once or twice
daily with meals, titrated up to reduce GI side effects.

3. Second-line options
When HbA1c remains above target on metformin monotherapy, consider adding
a GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor, particularly in patients with
established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney
disease, where these classes have demonstrated outcome benefits beyond
glycemic control.

4. Monitoring
HbA1c should be checked every 3 months until stable at target, then every
6 months. Target HbA1c is generally <7% for most adults, individualized
higher (e.g. <8%) for patients with limited life expectancy, extensive
comorbidity, or history of severe hypoglycemia.

5. Hypoglycemia management
Mild hypoglycemia (patient conscious, able to swallow): give 15-20g fast
acting carbohydrate, recheck glucose in 15 minutes, repeat if still low.
Severe hypoglycemia (altered consciousness): glucagon administration or
emergency services; do not give oral carbohydrate if the patient cannot
safely swallow.

6. Lifestyle counseling
All patients should receive counseling on carbohydrate-consistent eating
patterns, regular physical activity (at least 150 minutes/week of
moderate-intensity activity), and weight management where applicable.
